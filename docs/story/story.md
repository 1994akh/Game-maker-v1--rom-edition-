# Halcyra: the story

**Status:** first full version, 14 Oct 2026. It's built from your answers in the 14 Oct story Q&A (§13) and reuses
the best parts of `drafts/elemental-epic.md`. All names are invented. The hero's default name is **Rowan**, but the
player can rename them. Dialogue uses the `{HERO}` tokens. Anything that needs a new system or changes how the game
plays is listed in §14 with a recommendation, and is not decided here.

---

## 1. Logline, tone, themes

**Logline.** Halcyra's six elemental Wells were sealed six hundred years ago, and the world's edges have been
pouring into the void ever since. {HERO} is sworn to guard the Earth Well. They set out to catch Corvin, the
childhood friend everyone thought had drowned, who has come back to break the seals. Two crews sail an open sea
full of strange islands. Along the way they learn what the seals really do, who built them, and why the man who
guards them won't let go.

**Tone: warm adventure, real stakes.**
- The day-to-day mood is One Piece: jokes, crew banter, big emotions said out loud, and a ship that feels like home.
- At the big moments the weight comes from Golden Sun and Final Fantasy: a town falls, a mentor dies, and the
  villain is right about the problem.
- **No party member dies.** Towns, mentors and side characters can be lost.
- The fantasy stays lean, as in Star Trek. There's no prophecy and no chosen one. These are people on an
  interesting voyage, and they solve most problems by understanding them.

**Themes.**
- Duty against truth.
- Two sides that are each half right.
- Found family across a divide.
- Curiosity as a virtue: the log, first contact, the islands of the week.
- Legendaries that are wounded and opinionated, not evil.

## 2. The world: a map you can hold in your head

Halcyra is laid out in **rings around one continent**. The rings make the map easy to read: from anywhere, the
centre is "toward the Pinnacle" and the edge is "toward the falls".

   Layers:  ABOVE = the Cloudreach (cloud sea)   BELOW = the Deep Shelf   BESIDE = the Hollow (Giratina's mirror world)

| Ring | What it is | How you reach it | What gates it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridia**, the continent | Four walkable regions around the **Spine**, a mountain range with the capital, **the Pinnacle**, at its peak. **Brightcoast** (east: farms, canals), **the Greenwild** (west: the giant tree), **Dustplain** (south: desert, mines) and **Frostmarch** (north: tundra, monasteries) | On foot from the coast | Puzzles and danger; the Pinnacle is closed by story until Act III |
| **The Calm**, the inner sea | A gentle ring of islands: the home island, the volcano, the reef, the fortress | A cutter (from mission 3) | Calm water. A few whirlpools and reefs are puzzle gates |
| **The Galeway** | A permanent storm ring | Act I: only Rimborn skiffs know the lanes (Crew 2's interludes). Act II: it breaks into passable fragments | Story gate, then strong monsters in the storm fragments |
| **The Wide**, the outer sea | Four quarters (Northreach, Eastwater, Southdeep, Westgloam), each with its own look, weather and Pokémon culture | The **Wayfarer** (from mission 10) | Strong monsters (danger shown on the map); puzzles; Stillwater calm belts need the Flame engine |
| **The Rimfalls** | The world's edge, where the sea pours away | Visible from the outer sea | Currents push ships back; it can't be crossed |
| **Layers** | The Cloudreach above and the Deep Shelf below, **each about the size of one quarter of the Wide** (decided 14 Oct), with their own towns, routes and islands of the week; the Hollow beside | Updraft and wings; Dive and the bubble hull; the Vault tear | Story and site rewards |

**One tall landmark per region**, so you can always steer by sight:
- the Pinnacle spire (centre, visible almost everywhere);
- Embral's volcano plume (south);
- the Eldergrove tree (west);
- the Stormspire, a tower that pierces the clouds (north);
- the Silverdeep whirl, a giant whirlpool with a lighthouse (south-east);
- the black falls of the Umbral Gate (north-west rim);
- Marrow Isle's beacon (home, east).

**What can block a route.** Only three things do, as you asked:

1. **Puzzles:** a field ability you don't have yet.
2. **Story:** the Galeway in Act I, Tidewarden blockades, and the Pinnacle until Act III.
3. **Power:** monsters and wild Pokémon far above your level. They are always shown on the map before you go
   (§10), and a brave player can push through.

There are no invisible walls and no level scaling.

## 3. The conflict, in three layers

Your answer combined all the options, so the conflict peels back in three layers. Each layer is the truth of the act
it belongs to.

| Layer | What the player believes | Act |
|---|---|---|
| **1. An order vs free islands** | The **Tidewardens** rule the sea lanes from the Pinnacle. They license every Adept and Pokémon handler, collect an "element tithe", and blockade islands that won't pay. The free islands and the Rimborn resist | Prologue and Act I |
| **2. The elements are failing** | The seals don't hold the Wells back. They drain the Wells up the Spine into **the Crown**, a lattice under the Pinnacle that keeps Meridia green while the rim starves and falls. The legendaries have been drained for six centuries, which is why each one wakes in pain | Act I's ending (the Ledger) and Act II |
| **3. Someone built this** | Six hundred years ago, when the world was already dying once, the **Farers** came over the edge in ships that sailed the sky: outsiders from beyond the Rimfalls. They built the Crown as a stopgap meant to last one generation, left the Wayfarer, the Lodestar compass and a half-written **Farer's Log**, and sailed away promising to return. The Tidewarden order was founded to "hold until they return" | Revealed at the Stormspire (Act II); pays off in the postgame |

### The villain: Lord Warden Aurelian Vey (sympathetic, and he has a point)

- **What he knows:** Vey has read the Farers' instructions. The Crown was meant to be unwound gradually.
- **What he fears:** unwinding it now means the heartland of Meridia, home to millions, loses its endless summer
  and some of it floods.
- **What he has done:** he has kept the Crown running and waited for the Farers, while the rim fell a street at a
  time.
- **His last resort:** wake Groudon with the Red Orb and raise the Spine. That saves the centre and lets the edges
  go.
- **Why he's sympathetic:** the people he protects are real and on screen. He isn't cruel, he's frightened and
  faithful. He's right that opening every Well at once would be a catastrophe, and that's exactly what the Breakers
  want.
- **His end:** beaten at the Crown's root, he understands. He stays in the Hollow to hold its gate shut while the
  Crown unwinds. He isn't killed and isn't forgiven, but he's redeemed (§11).

### The other forces

| Faction | Leaders | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidewardens** | Vey; **Commodore Ilse Graymantle**, the hero's patron, who believes in the order and doesn't know the truth | To keep the seals |
| **Rimborn** (refugees from the falling rim) | **Captain Sael**, Corvin's foster mother (moderates) | To open the Wells one at a time |
| **The Breakers** (Rimborn radicals) | **Hask** | To tear out every seal at once. They cause the midpoint disaster |
| **Free islands** | Many: the Tradewind Bazaar, monasteries, pirates, odd cultures | Mostly to be left alone. They become the crews' allies, one island at a time |
| **The Farers** (outsiders) | Gone. Their caretaker mind, **the Steward**, still runs the Stormspire | To come back "when the Log is complete". The postgame |

- **Commodore Ilse:** the hero's mentor. She is put on trial in Act III and survives.
- **Captain Sael:** dies holding the Fallhaven archive in mission 9. This is the story's big loss.

**Popular-10 roles:**
- Hask's ace is a Weyard Mega Garchomp, and a wild Gible is a chase encounter on Dunesea.

- Eevee's other branches appear across the Wide as Djinn-style finds.

## 5. The legendaries

As you asked, all three roles are combined. Every legendary **judges** the crew, several **talk** and take sides, and
**all six can be caught**, each in a different way. Calming one unlocks its group's top Summon at once. Catching it
is a separate later beat, so a legendary never joins the party early enough to break the balance (§14, question 9).

| Legendary | Site | Role in the story | When it's calmed (Summon) | How it's caught |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ho-Oh** | Forgeheart, Embral Isle (Flame) | **Judge.** It wakes in agony when Corvin pulls the Flame seal and tests Crew 2 in battle | Mission 4 | **After a major story point:** after the Breaking it nests in Embral's new caldera, and you fight and catch it there |
| **Celebi** | Eldergrove Heart (Forest) | **Talks** through time visions: it showed the founders sealing the Wells and has been trying to warn people ever since | Mission 14 | **Puzzle:** finish the past/present garden chain in Root and Ruin and it comes to you |
| **Lugia** | Silverdeep (Sea) | **Takes a side.** It sided with the Tidewardens, believing the seals protect the sea, and has to be convinced by evidence, not just beaten | Mission 18 | **End of its arc:** after Silverdeep it waits at the Silverdeep lighthouse and joins when you return |
| **Rayquaza** | Stormspire (Sky) | **Judge and guardian** of the Farers' tower; it dives in to stop Primal Groudon in Act III | Mission 19 | **Classic:** a battle-and-catch at the Stormspire summit, reachable by airship after its arc |
| **Groudon** | Marrow Vault (Earth) | Vey forces it into its **Primal** form with the Red Orb. It's the Act III catastrophe | Mission 22 | **End of the story arc:** it rests in the Vault after the ending; caught in the postgame |
| **Giratina** | The Hollow (Shadow) | **Talks and sides with Vey:** it offered the Hollow as a refuge for Meridia's people. It's the final judge | Mission 23 | **Classic, postgame:** a superboss-strength battle-and-catch deep in the Hollow |

## 6. How the story opens up

The story structure is fixed by the brief and mapped onto your "short guided intro, then open" choice:

- **Prologue (guided, about 2–3 hours, missions 1–4):**
  - the hero alone against the rival;
  - a mission played as the rival;
  - Maren joins and the cutter opens the Calm;
  - a Crew 2 interlude at the Flame site.
- **Act I, Open Water (open):**
  - You play Crew 1 freely across Meridia and the Calm.
  - The Lodestar offers story bearings, but you can ignore them and explore.
  - Crew 1's two recruitments can be done in either order.
  - Each one is followed by a short Crew 2 interlude where the rest of Crew 2 joins.
  - The act ends when both crews meet at Fallhaven and find the Wayfarer. **All eight Adepts are recruited, so
    crew swapping unlocks** (the brief's rule).
- **Midpoint, the Breaking (FF6-style world change):** on the Wayfarer's maiden voyage, the Breakers blow the
  Galeway relay.
  - The storm ring shatters and the Calm's water level rises.
  - Low islands sink, sea-floor ruins rise as new islands, and the rim falls faster.
  - The crews are scattered across the map.
- **Act II, the Wide World (fully open):** the hero and Corvin regroup at home. Swapping is back on, and the whole
  sea is open.
  - The six regroup chapters, three Well sites and dozens of islands can be done in almost any order.
  - The Stormspire gives the ship **wings**: the late-game airship, with places only reachable from the air.
- **Act III, the Crown (linear finale):** the Pinnacle, the Marrow Vault and the Hollow.
- **Ending and postgame:** the ending varies with your heart quests and the islands you helped (§11). The postgame
  covers the Farers' return, superbosses and legendary catches.

## 7. Mission list

The **Crew** column says which crew you play. "Both" means both crews are on site and **relay stones** (restoration
points inside the dungeon) switch control between them.

The **Gate** column says what keeps the place closed before its time:
- **Open:** you can walk in as soon as you can reach it.
- **Puzzle:** it needs a field ability.
- **Story:** it's closed until a story flag.
- **Danger:** it's reachable, but strong.

From mission 5 on, mandatory puzzles use only Crew 1's Psynergy (Earth, Sea, Forest, Shadow) or what the named
Adept brings. Crew 2-only verbs (Blaze, Whirlwind, Mind Read) guard optional rewards until swapping unlocks,
because Act I is played as Crew 1.

## 8. Home, the ship and the log (the Star Trek layer)

- **Home island: Marrow Isle.** It's a Suikoden-style base that **grows**:
  - Fallhaven's refugees settle there in mission 10.
  - People rescued on other islands move in and open shops, a shipwright, a Pokémon daycare, a library of
    Farer texts, and the **Kin Garden** (below).
  - Crew swapping and the cosmetics mirror are here (both already decided).
- **The Kin Garden: where your extra Pokémon live.** This is the home of the shared Pokémon storage the party rules
  already define:
  - The party limit stays as decided: each Adept carries **3 Set Pokémon**.
  - Every Pokémon caught or joined beyond that goes to the Kin Garden on Marrow Isle, with the usual "sent to
    storage" message.
  - You see them there: they roam a garden that grows terraces, ponds and caves as it fills, and you can talk to
    them.
  - They stay there until you switch them in. Under the decided rule, Set Pokémon change at **restoration points**,
    and every restoration point reaches the Kin Garden's storage. Question 20 in §14 asks whether you'd rather
    allow swaps only on the home island.
- **The ship: the Wayfarer.** It's the mobile hub:
  - crew quarters, where you talk to each Adept between missions and their heart quests start;
  - a galley for crew-banter scenes;
  - a chart room with the Lodestar.
- **Ship upgrades come from opened Wells:**

  | Well | Upgrade | What it does |
  |---|---|---|
  | Flame | Engine | Crosses the Stillwaters |
  | Forest | Living hull | Sails up rivers |
  | Sea | Bubble hull | Sails the Deep Shelf |
  | Sky | Wings | The airship |

  - From mission 19 the Wayfarer flies over everything.
  - Places only reachable from the air: the Spine's peaks, sky isles, Rimfall ledges and an island inside the old
    Galeway eye.
  - Several superbosses live there.
- **The Farer's Log, an exploration log** (a Farer book the Steward helps you fill in). It has four sections:

  | Section | What it records |
  |---|---|
  | **Kin** (the Pokédex) | Every Pokémon, as **unseen / seen / caught** |
  | **Bestiary** | Golden Sun monsters and bosses, as **seen / defeated**. They can't be caught |
  | **Charts** | Islands, regions, landmarks and hidden places, as **rumoured / visited / fully explored** |
  | **Relics and Cultures** | Farer relics, ruins and the island cultures you made contact with |

- **What a Kin entry shows:**
  - its Weyard form or ordinary form, its types, and which of the six elemental groups it belongs to;
  - where it lives, marked on the world map (habitat and time of day);
  - how it's met: wild, Djinn-style (joins, chases, battles first, or hidden), legendary or gift;
  - its size (1 slot, or 2 for large species) and its evolution line;
  - the field abilities (HMs) it can learn, so you know who can Surf, Dive or Cut;
  - which Adept it's Set on, or that it's in the Kin Garden.
- **The Kin entry text** is written in your crew's voices. Whoever first saw the Pokémon writes its entry: Sorrel is
  tender with Forest species, Tamsin rates how hard they hit, Wynn draws a tiny map. Caught entries add a line from
  its Adept.
- **Captain's log:** each Charts entry gets a short "captain's log" line in the voice of whichever lead is
  captaining.
- **Rewards:** filling each section gives gear, rumours and Kin Garden decorations.
- **The Farers' return:** completing Charts and Relics triggers it (§11). Completing Kin gives its own reward, the
  postgame Mew sighting.
- **Islands of the week (§9):** self-contained stories with a moral or cultural puzzle, solved by talking,
  thinking or fighting. Many are first contact with odd cultures, misunderstood creatures or Farer machines.

## 9a. Easter eggs, homages and spoof cameos

The game's influences show up as easter eggs: some small (a line of dialogue, a poster, a statue), some large (a
whole side quest). The aim is "obvious if you know it, invisible if you don't".

**Copyright-friendly rules** (they also apply to the brief's rule against using these franchises' names):
- Every spoof has an **original name and look**. It's recognisable by role, silhouette, personality and running
  gags, not by a copied name, costume or logo.
- **Catchphrases are bent, never quoted.** For example, "Make it flow", not the original line.
- No copied music, art or logos.
- Pokémon species names are fine (already decided).

### Themed puzzles

**One Piece puzzles**
1. **"The Wrong Way"** (Torro's escort): the Lodestar points one way and Torro walks the other. Following him on
   purpose through a hedge maze or a fog bank reveals hidden paths only his "wrong" route opens.
2. **"The Captain's Lunch"** (Maize's feast): a food chase across a lashed-ship deck. Move crates, Frost puddles
   into stepping stones and Lash ropes to reach meat-on-the-bone before his crew does.
3. **"Eight Thousand Followers"** (Captain Fibber): a logic puzzle. Some of his tall tales are secretly true, and
   only the true ones mark a dig spot on the map (Scoop).
4. **"Bounty Board":** every Tidewarden notice board shows wanted posters of your crew. The bounties rise as you
   defy the order, and the posters use terrible drawings (one Adept always gets the worst portrait). The posters
   are part of the bounty system in §9b.
5. **"A Funeral at Sea"** (an emotional egg): when the Wayfarer replaces the old cutter *Kestrel*, an optional scene
   gives it a fiery send-off at sea.

**Star Trek puzzles**
1. **"The Speakers of Tanarra"** (an island of the week): a people who speak only in metaphors from their legends.
   Learn each image from murals around the island, then answer them in their own language to open the shrine. "The
   tide, when the cliff fell."
2. **"The Unwinnable Test"** (Aerie Monastery): a Farer battle trial that can't be won as set up. The solution is
   to change the rules: Move the arena's pillars before the fight so the enemy can't reach you.
3. **"The Trouble with Wooloo"** (a risen isle): a cute Pokémon herd that doubles every time you rest, overrunning
   a village. Find the one food that calms them, while the Tumbleweed Trio try to sell them.
4. **"The Hive"** (Eldergrove's bug hive, mission 5): a Combee swarm that "absorbs" everything that enters. Cloak

5. **"Energize":** the Stormspire's Teleport pads are a puzzle of pad pairs. Each pad hums a different tone, and
   stepping on the wrong pair scatters the crew around the floor.
6. **"The Dreamdeck":** a Farer room in the Stormspire that rebuilds earlier places from your Log. It's a puzzle

## 9b. Bounties and generated contracts (outside the story)

Two kinds of bounty, One Piece style, plus **generated side quests** so there's always something to do, before and
after the ending.

### Your crew's bounty (being wanted)

- **The posters:** every Adept has a bounty, shown on wanted posters with terrible portraits (§9a). The figures
  rise at story beats where you defy the Tidewardens, and from big contract wins.
- **What it does:** a higher bounty unlocks higher contract tiers at the free ports. It also sends **rival hunter
  crews** after you (below).
- **What it doesn't do:** it never blocks a town or raises prices. Tidewarden towns just add a joke line.
- **After the ending:** the order is reformed and the posters change to "Wardens of the Wells: reward for
  information". The figure becomes your fame.

### Hunting bounties (the boards)

Every free port has a **Bounty Board** run by the **Freeport Guild**, a guild of the free islands. Contracts come in
two flavours:

| Kind | What it is | How many |
|---|---|---|
| **Named bounties** | Hand-written: a notorious monster, a bandit captain, a cameo character's request. Each has a short story and a unique reward | About 30 across the game; some unlock only after the Breaking or the ending |
| **Generated contracts** | Built from templates and the game's own data, so the board always has fresh work | Unlimited; each board shows 3–5 at a time and refreshes when you rest at a restoration point |

**Guild rank** (from Bronze to Legend) rises with completed contracts and gates the harder tiers, together with your
crew's bounty. A Guild Ledger page in the Farer's Log tracks contracts done, rank and named bounties.

### How generated contracts are built

A contract is a **template** whose slots are filled from data the game already has:

| Template | The goal | Filled from | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunt** | Defeat a monster (a stronger "alpha" variant) in a region | The Bestiary, the region's encounter table and danger level | "An alpha Chimera Worm is attacking ferries off Dustplain. Reward: 1,200 coins." |
| **Catch** | Catch a Pokémon of a given species, type or trait | Kin data and habitats | "A Pearlhollow dome-keeper needs a Water-type that knows Dive." |
| **Survey** | Visit or fully explore a place, or find a rumoured island | Charts and rumours | "Chart the risen isle south-west of Embral." |
| **Salvage** | Recover an item from a wreck, ruin or Dive spot | Item tables and Dive spots | "Raise a Farer relic from the Kelpwood wreck." |
| **Delivery and escort** | Carry goods or a passenger between two ports in time | The port list and sailing routes | "Take medicine to Hushwater before the next rest." |
| **Clear** | Break up a bandit camp or a Tumbleweed Trio scheme | Bandit groups and camp locations | "Bandits have taken the lighthouse on Twin Bells." |
| **Puzzle cache** | Solve a small generated puzzle room for a stash | Puzzle room kits for each field ability | "A Farer vault on Thunderhead opens to Frost and Move." |

**The rules that keep generated contracts sane:**
- **Only reachable places:** a contract only names places you can already reach with your current ship upgrades and
  field abilities. A higher tier may name places with high danger stars, and the board shows those stars.
- **Reward scales with danger and rank:** coins, items, Kin Garden decorations, and now and then a rare Pokémon
  encounter, a backup Psynergy item or a rumour that adds a "?" to the map.
- **Flavour text is assembled from pieces:** the client, the problem and a twist, from pools for each region, so a
  Frostmarch contract reads differently from a Southdeep one. One crew member comments on each contract in their
  own voice.
- **Never the main story:** generated contracts don't touch story flags and never compete with story bosses or
  legendaries.

### Rival hunter crews

These are bounty hunters who come after **you**:
- **Who they are:** small, generated crews. Each has a captain name from a pool, an element theme and an Adept +
  Pokémon roster built like the trainer-class rules.
- **How they meet you:** they show up when your crew's bounty crosses a threshold. They ambush you at sea or on a
  route, or challenge you at a port.
- **Beating one** lowers your heat for a while, and some of them later become friendly contacts.
- **The cameos:** the Tumbleweed Trio count as a hunter crew, a very bad one.

## 10. Navigation aids (all four you picked)

- **Danger on the map:** each region and island shows 1–5 stars once it's known (the Danger column in §9). That's
  the "too strong" warning, given before you go.
- **Landmarks you can see from far away:** one tall landmark per region (§2). The camera on the world map is pulled
  back enough to see the nearest one.
- **The Lodestar compass:**
  - By default it points at the next main-story goal.
  - Tap it to point at a marker or rumour you chose instead.
  - It's a Farer device: it "wakes" in stages, which the story uses (mission 12 is its first full use).
- **Rumours:**
  - NPCs, tavern boards and Log entries add **"?" markers** to the map.
  - A "?" turns into the place's name and danger stars once you visit or someone describes it in detail.
  - Every island of the week starts as a rumour.

**The guest system (question 3), as I'd specify it:**
- **When it applies:** after the Breaking, an Adept found by the other crew joins it as a **guest**.
- **What a guest does:** a guest fills an empty slot (scattered crews are under 4, so there's room), fights with
  their own Set Pokémon, and lends their field Psynergy.
- **When it ends:** the guest goes back to their own crew when you reach Marrow Isle, or straight away if their own
  crew's regroup chapter starts.
- **What a guest can't do:** guests can't be moved between crews or have their Set Pokémon changed. That's still
  the home-island and restoration-point rule.
- **Why it's this small:** it's the smallest version of the system. It reuses the crew's 4 slots instead of adding a
  fifth.

The original questions and recommendations follow, for reference.

Each question has my recommendation.

1. **Crew 2 interludes in an open Act I.**
   - **Recommendation:** a Crew 2 interlude triggers at the next restoration point after each Crew 1 recruitment
     (missions 7 and 8). Crew 1's progress pauses and resumes where it left off.
   - **Why:** recruitment stays free in order, and the brief's "story picks the crew until swapping" holds.
2. **Walking on the world map.**
   - **Recommendation:** yes on Meridia and the large islands, Golden Sun style. Small islands are entered
     straight from the ship.
   - **Why:** "one continent you can walk" was the shape you picked.
3. **Scattered crews after the Breaking (missions 12–20).**
   - **Recommendation:** crews can be smaller than 4 (already allowed). A missing Adept is simply absent until
     their regroup chapter, and the regroup chapter adds them back to their own crew.
   - **Why:** no guest system is needed.
4. **Bags before the crews unite.**
   - **Recommendation:** each crew has its own bag and coins until mission 10, then they merge. Pokémon storage is
     always shared.
   - **Why:** this is the draft's answer, and it still fits.
5. **Map changes at the midpoint.**
   - **Recommendation:** map variants switched by flag: Canalport (flooded), three sunk isles, three risen isles,
     the Galeway as fragments, and the rim edge moved inward. There's no timer.
   - **Why:** a world change with no new system.
6. **The airship.**
   - **Recommendation:** the ship flies over the world map with its own collision layer and lands only on marked
     pads. It doesn't fly inside towns or dungeons.
   - **Why:** this is the FF model, and it keeps area design simple.
7. **The Cloudreach and the Deep Shelf.**
   - **Recommendation:** one small sailable chunk each. Dive spots lead to on-foot underwater maps.
   - **Why:** variety without a second world.
8. **The Hollow (a mirror world).**
   - **Recommendation:** recoloured copies of 5–6 earlier maps with new puzzles.
   - **Why:** cheap content and a Golden Sun-style final dungeon.
9. **Legendary catches mid-game** (Ho-Oh after the Breaking, Celebi, Lugia, Rayquaza in Act II).
   - **Recommendation:** they join at the level of the area where they are caught, and can only be Set, never be
     the active partner, until Act III.
   - **Why:** this keeps the "catchable at different times" fun without trivialising Act II.
10. **The Lodestar, rumours and danger stars.**
    - **Recommendation:** world-map UI only. Rumours are flags set by dialogue, and danger is a per-area number set
      by hand.
    - **Why:** no new gameplay system.
11. **Allies at the Pinnacle (mission 21).**
    - **Recommendation:** each helped island adds a one-time battle assist (an item drop, a free Summon charge or a
      distraction that removes one enemy slot) in the Pinnacle fights.
    - **Why:** side stories pay off in play, not only in the montage.
12. **The time loop (Clockwork Key).**
    - **Recommendation:** one map with a "loop count" flag. Changes you make persist across loops only where the
      script says so.
    - **Why:** map state by flag, with no new system.
13. **Talking a fight down (Hushwater, the Sentinel, Lugia).**
    - **Recommendation:** a dialogue choice before or during a scripted battle that ends it, with no new battle
      rules.
    - **Why:** it carries the Star Trek "understanding, not fighting" theme cheaply.
14. **The final battle relay.**
    - **Recommendation:** Vey fights in two phases, one per crew, in the order the player picks. His HP carries
      over between phases.
    - **Why:** the two-crew theme pays off at the end.
15. **The postgame (the Farers' return).**
    - **Recommendation:** a small set of new islands and superbosses plus the remaining legendary catches. No new
      systems.
    - **Why:** it reuses systems that already exist.
16. **The Farer's Log** (four sections, §8).
    - **Recommendation:** a menu screen with the four sections. Kin entries are filled from the species data
      (types, group, size, evolution, learnable field abilities) plus a hand-written text per species. Habitat
      marks come from the encounter tables. "Seen" is set when a species first appears in battle or as a
      Djinn-style encounter on the field. Each section shows a completion percentage and has rewards at
      thresholds.
    - **Why:** the Pokédex and the Star Trek log in one book, and it drives exploration.
17. **The bounty system (§9b).** You asked for a real bounty system, so this replaces the earlier "cosmetic
    only" recommendation.
    - **Recommendation:** build it in three steps:
      1. Hand-written contracts from the boards.
      2. Generated contracts from templates (§9b).
      3. Rival hunter crews, last.
    - The crew's own bounty only unlocks contract tiers and hunter ambushes. It never raises prices or blocks
      towns.
    - **Why:**
      - Step 1 is ordinary quest scripting.
      - Step 2 is the only genuinely new system: it fills templates from data that already exists (encounter
        tables, danger levels, item and NPC lists).
      - Step 3 is optional.
18. **Mini-games from the easter eggs** (the Dodo Derby, the Glimmer Saucer, the Speakers' language).
    - **Recommendation:** build only the Speakers' puzzle (it's dialogue plus murals). The Derby and the casino are
      postgame extras, if time allows.
    - **Why:** they're new systems and the least important part of the game.
19. **The world after the ending (§11).** **Decided 14 Oct: yes, as recommended.**
    - **Recommendation:** a post-ending flag that switches the world to its final map variants (the receding
      rim, regrown isles, changed NPC dialogue) and returns you to Marrow Isle with everything else intact.
      Optional content never checks "before the ending".
    - **Why:** it's the same map-variant-by-flag tool as the midpoint, and it keeps every catch and side quest
      reachable.
20. **Where you can swap Pokémon in from the Kin Garden.** **Decided 14 Oct: keep the current rule** (any
    restoration point).
    - **Recommendation:** keep the decided rule: at any restoration point, which reaches the Kin Garden's storage.
      The Kin Garden on Marrow Isle is where they live and where you can see them.
    - **Alternative:** swaps only on the home island. That's stricter and more Golden Sun-like, but slower once
      the map is large.
    - **Why:** you asked for the established rules, and they already say restoration points.
